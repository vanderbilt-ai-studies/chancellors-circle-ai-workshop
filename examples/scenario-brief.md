# Example only: advisory-firm scenario brief

Industry: professional services | Approach: simulation | Horizon: 2030

## Summary

In this fictional example, faster drafting does not automatically mean more completed client work. The prepared model makes senior review the bottleneck: output falls from 12 to eight briefs under the initial agent assumptions. If review time can fall without losing quality, capacity could rise to 16. These are conditional calculations, not measured business results. People still need to understand clients, verify claims, and own the outcome. A small pilot measuring complete-workflow time and quality would help test the main uncertainty: whether reliable review can scale.

## 1. The change we investigated
Could a small firm use agents to serve more clients with public-information briefs? Our starting hypothesis was that faster drafts would increase completed work. This is a fictional demonstration, not a record of a table's views.

## 2. What we found happening already
The original 2023 NBER *Generative AI at Work* working-paper version reported roughly 14% more issues resolved per hour with an assistant in customer support ([original paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/revisions/w31161.rev0.pdf)). METR's July 10, 2025 developer experiment found a 19% slowdown in a different setting ([report](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). These prepared summaries were not rechecked live in this example. Neither establishes outcomes for an advisory firm or today's autonomous agents.

## 3. A plausible future and its assumptions
By 2030 the firm might sell a lower-cost brief service if agents reduce preparation time, clients value the product, and reliable review scales. In the fictional model, halving analyst time from eight to four hours while increasing review from one to 1.5 hours reduces completed briefs from 12 to eight per week. Changing only review to 0.75 hours raises capacity to 16. These are conditional calculations, not measured gains.

A slower future retains draft assistance because review and trust remain limiting. A useful sign would be more completed briefs at unchanged verified quality during a real pilot. The model omits costs, queues, and rework; it cannot establish profitability.

## 4. What people and organizations would still contribute
People would frame client questions, evaluate sources, resolve ambiguities, maintain relationships, and own the result. A graduate may need to verify agent output and identify workflow bottlenecks.

## 5. Our biggest disagreement or uncertainty
We could disagree about whether improved source packets reduce review time. A small comparison of similar briefs, with a common review checklist and time recording, would help. A proposed pilot is not proof.

## 6. What changed in our thinking
Before: faster drafts might mean more clients. Now: that depends on the rest of the workflow and actual demand. This is an example reflection for the fictional case.

Report-out: We explored agent-assisted briefs. We were surprised by the review bottleneck. Our biggest uncertainty is whether review can become faster without losing quality.
