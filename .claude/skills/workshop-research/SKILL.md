# Research: is this really happening?

Read `docs/facilitation.md` first. It sets the one-question-at-a-time interaction, evidence labels, switching behavior, and four-minute wrap-up. All paths below are relative to the repository root. Use the supplied topic and time; ask only for what is missing. Ask for the table's initial expectation before doing analysis. If no topic is chosen, offer `materials/advisory-firm-case.md` and wait for agreement.

The student keeps time. The schedule below totals 20 minutes including a first brief. At four minutes remaining, skip unfinished exploration and draft. Do not run all the steps in one answer.

## 0–2 minutes: make one claim testable

Help narrow the proposal to one task, organization type, and outcome. Example: “Agent-assisted public research could help a small advisory firm serve more clients.” Ask which outcome matters most. Record their expectation and one thing that would challenge it.

## 2–7 minutes: find a little good evidence

If web access works, search once for evidence supporting the claim and once for failures, null results, or limitations. Seek up to three original sources: measured results where available, a concrete deployment account, and a meaningful challenge. Open sources; search snippets alone are not verification. Label vendor claims. Record source title, date, URL, setting, result, and boundary. Repeated coverage of one study is one source.

Stop searching after five minutes, two unsuccessful tool calls, or three useful sources, whichever comes first. Say what remains missing. If tools are unavailable, immediately use `materials/source-cards.md`; say these are prepared summaries, not newly checked sources. In another industry, identify the relevance gap. Never invent an industry example to fill it.

Show a small table: claim | what the source supports | what it does not establish. Ask: “Which result is most relevant to your work?” Wait.

## 7–12 minutes: challenge the inference

Show the strongest limitation or contrary finding. Ask: “What would make our comparison misleading?” Distinguish today's observed task assistance from a future end-to-end agent workflow. Do not average incompatible studies, infer causality from vendor anecdotes, or treat missing evidence as disproof. Make one revised, conditional claim.

## 12–16 minutes: follow the change outward

Offer a short chain: task change -> workflow change -> service or competitive consequence. Mark the last links as hypotheses. Ask what people still supply. Then ask what changed in the table's thinking, in a separate turn. Preserve a disagreement or evidence gap.

## 16–20 minutes: write the brief

Use `templates/scenario-brief.md`. Include the best evidence with boundaries, a plausible future, assumptions, the strongest challenge, and one observation that could change the conclusion. Save or display the brief as the shared rules require.

Collect the table's ideas into a succinct one-paragraph summary and review it together. Ask the table to name itself. Follow `docs/report-out.md` to save `report-out/[table-name]-[industry]-analysis.md`; prompt the student to provide the chosen name and ask for the reviewed results to be committed and pushed. Publish only after that request, and return the verified report link.

## Basis

This is a **workshop evidence scan**, not a systematic or formal rapid review. Adapted principles: focused question, explicit selection limits, source checking, and transparent uncertainty. See the research entry in `docs/methods-and-sources.md` for the Cochrane methods source and the limits of this adaptation.
