# Forecasting: how might the future unfold?

This is an optional extension. The workshop begins with research, simulation, or role playing; use this extension when the table requests it.

Read `docs/facilitation.md` first. It sets the one-question-at-a-time interaction, evidence labels, switching behavior, and four-minute wrap-up. All paths below are relative to the repository root. Use the supplied topic and time; ask only for what is missing. Ask for the table's initial expectation before doing analysis. If no topic is chosen, offer `materials/advisory-firm-case.md` and wait for agreement.

The student keeps time. The schedule below totals 20 minutes including a first brief. At four minutes remaining, skip unfinished exploration and draft. Do not run all the steps in one answer.

## 0–2 minutes: make the future concrete

Confirm one industry, one change, and a year three to five years from the session (2030 is a convenient default for October 2026). Ask which observable outcome matters: adoption of a specific workflow, a changed service, or another clear result. Avoid “AI transforms everything.”

## 2–6 minutes: identify what could change the path

Offer up to three uncertainties: reliable performance, access to information, customer trust, costs, or implementation capacity. Mark them as proposed drivers, not facts. Ask which is most consequential and genuinely uncertain. Use a relevant source card or table experience to anchor today's picture, labeling the basis. Do not claim the baseline was researched when it was not.

## 6–11 minutes: compare two futures

Build a compact table of a faster-change and slower-change future. For both use the same horizon and include: what changed in the work, what had to be true, who benefits or bears costs, and what people still do. Slower adoption may be rational; faster adoption may bring failures. Avoid simply calling one good and one bad. These are conditional scenarios, not probabilities or validated forecasts.

Ask: “Which assumption separates these futures most?” Revise with the table's expertise. Include one short causal chain from task to workflow to service or competitive change.

## 11–14 minutes: name signs to watch

Propose two observable signposts tied to the chosen uncertainty. For each specify what is measured, where a person could obtain it, when to check, and which future the observation would support. Label suggested thresholds as table hypotheses, not established benchmarks. A possible signal for the prepared case is reviewed briefs per week at unchanged quality in a documented pilot; a vague statement such as “AI gets better” is not a signpost.

Ask which sign would make the table change its view. Do not invent future data.

## 14–16 minutes: test robustness

Suggest one action useful in both futures, such as developing source-checking ability, and invite a better one. Ask what changed in their thinking. Preserve disagreement about pace or feasibility.

## 16–20 minutes: write the brief

Use `templates/scenario-brief.md`. Capture both futures briefly, their separating assumption, signposts, a robust action, and the main uncertainty. Do not report a numerical probability unless a participant supplies one, in which case label it that person's judgment rather than a calibrated estimate.

## Basis

This is an introductory scenario comparison informed by UK Government Office for Science Futures Toolkit, not a full foresight process or a statistical forecasting exercise. We shorten the usual scenario development and use two contrasting paths instead of a full matrix. See `docs/methods-and-sources.md`.
