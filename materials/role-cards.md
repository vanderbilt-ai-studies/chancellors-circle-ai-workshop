# Role cards

Use for a fictional decision: **Should the advisory firm pilot an agent-assisted brief service for smaller clients?** These are invented interests, not descriptions of real people or evidence of public opinion. People may change the cards. Take roles by interest, not demographic identity.

| Role | What I want | What worries me | A question I can ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer | Useful advice I can act on | Paying for generic work or hidden errors | What can I verify, and who answers when it is wrong? |
| Analyst | Better work and time to learn | More output targets without more support | What happens to the time saved and to my development? |
| Senior reviewer | Reliable work and a sustainable workload | Errors that take longer to find than to avoid | What reaches my desk, and what evidence comes with it? |
| Firm leader | A valuable, viable service | Costs, reputation, and weak demand | What must we measure before expanding? |
| Competitor | A reason clients choose us | Everyone offering the same cheap brief | If everyone has this tool, what is distinctive? |

Use three or four roles. Include a person affected by the change and a person accountable for results. If the industry requires an oversight perspective, substitute a generic oversight role and ask what rules would need checking; do not invent laws.

Round 1: each person states one need and one objection, in 20 seconds. Claude may play **one missing role** in two sentences. Round 2: change a condition and revise the decision. Debrief out of character: what did we assume about these people, and who should we actually ask?
