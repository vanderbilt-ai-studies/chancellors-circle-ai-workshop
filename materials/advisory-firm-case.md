# Prepared case: a small advisory firm

**Fictional case. Every operational number below is invented for the workshop. This is business-research work, not investment, legal, or clinical advice.**

A six-person firm prepares public-information market briefs for business clients. Each brief combines research, a draft, and senior review. The firm wonders whether agents could help it provide a useful service to smaller clients over the next three to five years.

**Starting question:** Could the same team offer more briefs without reducing quality? How would that change its service, customers, or competition?

**Starting expectation to accept or replace:** “Faster drafting will let us serve more clients.” Record the table's own view first.

## Today: a deliberately small model

- Five analysts each have 24 hours per week for these briefs: **120 analyst hours** total.
- One senior reviewer has **12 hours per week** for review.
- Each brief takes **8 analyst hours**, then **1 review hour**.
- There is demand for **20 briefs per week**.
- Work is divisible for capacity planning; round completed weekly briefs down to whole numbers.

## Proposed change: assumptions to test

- Agent-assisted research and drafting reduce analyst time to **4 hours per brief**.
- Extra checking raises senior review to **1.5 hours per brief**.
- Staff and demand initially stay the same.
- Agent cost, training, implementation, queueing, and client acquisition are outside the simple model. Discuss them before claiming business value.

## Three things the table may change

1. Analyst hours per brief with agents.
2. Senior review hours per brief with agents.
3. Weekly demand.

The student can run `python3 scripts/simulate.py` or use `materials/simulation-worked-example.md` on paper.

## Other ways into this case

- **Research:** What evidence supports faster work, and what evidence cautions us?
- **Role playing:** Should the firm pilot this service for smaller clients?
- **Forecasting:** By 2030, will this remain draft assistance or become a trusted new service?
- **Problem solving:** How could the firm relieve the review bottleneck without accepting lower quality?
