# Evidence cards: use even without web access

Prepared October 15, 2026. These are short paraphrases of original sources, not full papers. If offline, cite the original URL and say “prepared summary; not rechecked live.” The empirical examples describe different tasks and older tools. They are not estimates of autonomous agents' performance in 2026 or proof about the fictional advisory firm.

## E1: benefits can depend on the worker and task

**Source:** Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond, *Generative AI at Work*, NBER Working Paper 31161, original 2023 working-paper version. [Original version](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/revisions/w31161.rev0.pdf); [paper record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161).

**Finding:** The original study of a conversational assistant in customer support reported roughly 14% more issues resolved per hour on average, with larger benefits for less experienced workers.

**Boundary:** One workplace and task setting; assisted support work, not an autonomous advisory service. Later paper versions may report revised estimates. Do not turn 14% into a universal productivity assumption.

**Ask:** Which parts of our work resemble this setting? Which do not?

## E2: perceived speed can differ from measured results

**Source:** METR, *Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity*, July 10, 2025. [Study report](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/).

**Finding:** In a randomized study of 16 experienced developers completing 246 tasks in familiar repositories, allowing early-2025 AI tools increased completion time by 19%. Developers' impressions were more positive than the measured result.

**Boundary:** Specific developers, repositories, tasks, and tools. This does not establish that AI slows all work, or describe today's tools.

**Newer context:** In its [February 24, 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/), METR reported that selection and measurement problems made its follow-up an unreliable estimate of current effects. It considered greater speedups plausible but could not reliably quantify them.

**Ask:** What would we measure rather than relying on how fast the work feels?

## E3: a skill can package a repeatable process

**Source:** Anthropic, *Equipping agents for the real world with Agent Skills*, October 16, 2025; updated December 18, 2025. [Engineering explanation](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills).

**Observation:** Skills package instructions and optional resources that an agent can load for a task.

**Boundary:** This is a vendor's description of a capability, not an independent evaluation of business outcomes. A skill cannot make its own claims true.

**Ask:** Which parts of our team's expertise could be written down and reused? Which require situated judgment?

## Use the cards well

E1 and E2 concern different settings: their results cannot be averaged or treated as a head-to-head comparison. E3 establishes a design pattern, not a return on investment. Together they motivate testing the whole workflow. If your industry is different, say that direct industry evidence remains missing. For a current-industry claim, find an appropriate primary source live or leave the claim open.
